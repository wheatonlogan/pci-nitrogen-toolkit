The relationship

Step 1  →  purity × pressure sets what one membrane makes.

Step 2  →  × membrane count gives the flow a Type delivers.

Step 3  →  flow × air factor = the feed air, which picks the compressor.

Purity appears in both lookups. That is why it moves the price twice — once by shrinking what a Type produces, again by raising the air each unit of nitrogen costs. Raising pressure does the opposite: more nitrogen per membrane and less air per unit. It costs compressor power instead.
